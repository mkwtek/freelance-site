Website accessibility audits in Seattle

Find out what's keeping people out of your website before a customer, a funder or a demand letter does. I test your site by hand and with tools, explain what I find in plain English, and help you fix the real problems.

What you get

  • Testing to WCAG 2.2 AA, the latest version of the accessibility standard. It includes everything in WCAG 2.1 AA, the version current laws point to.
  • Hands-on checks with only a keyboard, on real phones, with enlarged text, plus several automated tools. Automated scans alone catch only part of the problems.
  • A plain-English report that ranks what to fix first, and how.
  • Fixes, not overlays. I fix the code itself, or show your team how. Overlay widgets don't prevent lawsuits.

Who it's for

Small businesses open to the public, nonprofits and clinics funded by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (WCAG 2.1 AA required by May 2027, or May 2028 with fewer than 15 employees), and contractors who build content for state and local governments (April 2027).

Planning changes? Start here

If your site needs fixes or changes, an audit comes first. A quick patch on a site nobody has looked at can break something bigger, like a checkout page, a contact form or your search rankings. The audit shows how your site is built, what it depends on and where the risks are, so the fixes are planned and quoted before anything is touched. It works like a repair shop's diagnostic fee: you pay for the diagnosis, then decide what to fix based on what it finds and what it will cost.

What it costs

Three ways to start: a free site check of your home page (the three biggest fixes), a starter review for $100, with every fix your home page needs, or a full audit of the whole site: small sites up to 5 pages from $450, then $40 for each additional page, with pages that share a layout checked by sample. Typical full audits in the industry run $2,000 to $8,000. Your price depends on how many different page layouts and features your site has.
